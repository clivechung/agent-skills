# 🎯 Dialing-In Troubleshooting & Temperature Matrix

Reference guide for diagnosing flavor defects and selecting water temperatures based on bean roast and density.

---

## 1. "Dialing In" Troubleshooting Matrix

| Defect / Symptom | Root Cause | Remediation (Fix) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dry, bitter aftertaste (Astringency)** in 4:6 Method | Fines migrating to bottom and over-extracting during later pours, clogging flow. | Coarsen grind by **0.5** (e.g. 8.0 → 8.5); use low pour agitation in final stages. |
| **Bitter first sip with fast drawdown** (< 2:30) | Micro-channeling (grind slightly too fine causing water to rush through fast paths while over-extracting walls). | Coarsen grind slightly (e.g. 6.0 → 6.3) to balance bed resistance and stop channeling. |
| **Mild, thin, or hollow taste** | Brew ratio too diluted for bean solubility. | Tighten ratio from 1:15 to **1:14** (20g coffee to 280g water) to boost body and concentration. |

---

## 2. Temperature Selection Rulebook

Water temperature controls extraction rate and balances acidity vs. bitterness based on roast profile:

* **88°C**: Best for **high-altitude, dense washed beans** (e.g., *Nicaragua Organic*). Unlocks clean acidity and bright floral notes.
* **85°C ("Bingo Temp")**: Ideal for **medium-dark roasts** (e.g., *Ethiopia Pat Dark*, *Kenya Maasi Gold*). Delivers a long-lasting sweet aftertaste while suppressing harsh bitterness.
* **80°C – 84°C**: Best for **very dark, low-acidity blends** (e.g., *Musashino Mori*, *Roar Wolf*). Rounds out heavy roasty/chocolate notes and tames harsh tannins.
